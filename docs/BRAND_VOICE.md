# Brand Voice — Customer-Facing Copy

How Tools Australia sounds. Distilled from the May / July / August 2026 social ad scripts
(the voice that actually ran and converted), rewritten to be **rule 11 compliant**.

Use this when writing or reviewing any customer-facing string: landing/promo pages,
membership + pack UI, mini-draws, checkout & upsell, Cobber, Klaviyo/SendGrid emails,
SMS/push, SEO/meta.

> **Read `CLAUDE.md` rule 11 first.** It is the legal constraint; this doc is the style
> layer on top of it. Where they ever appear to conflict, **rule 11 wins.**
>
> The source ad scripts are **not** rule-11 clean — see [Source provenance](#source-provenance)
> at the bottom. Every example in this document has been checked. Copy from *here*, not
> from the PDFs.

---

## 1. The voice in one line

**A tradie mate who's in on the joke** — Australian, blunt, funny at its own expense, never
salesy-earnest. It sells the *fantasy of owning the gear*, not the mechanics of a promotion.

Three things it always does:

- **Talks to a person, not a market.** "Legends", "tradies", second person, singular.
- **Says the thing before you can.** It voices your objection, then flips it.
- **Ends in your garage.** Every close puts the prize in the reader's own space.

Three things it never does:

- Probability, luck, or odds framing (**legal** — see rule 11).
- Spec-sheet language in a hook. Specs are the payoff, never the opener.
- Corporate hedging — "we are pleased to offer", "exciting opportunity", "don't miss out on
  your chance to".

## 2. The patterns

Every example below is verbatim-or-lightly-cleaned from a script that ran, and is
rule-11 compliant as written.

### Pattern-interrupt negation
Deny something first, then pivot to the real prize. The single most-reused hook shape
across all three months.

> "You won't win me, but you might win this ultimate tradie setup."
> "This isn't Barbie's dreamhouse… it's Barbie's dream toolbox."

### Objection-flipping
Name the reader's resistance out loud, then answer it. Works on the prize itself, not just
on price.

> "Don't need new tools? Take home $10,000 cash instead."
> "You say it's too pink — I say you're too boring."
> "If your masculinity can't survive a pink toolbox, that is a YOU problem."

### Pre-empting the comment section
Self-aware, meta about its own ad. High-engagement device — it invites the argument it
predicts.

> "The comments section is about to be full of blokes saying that they wouldn't use this."
> "Pink, practical and somehow still tougher than the Tools Australia comment section."

### Anti-hype hooks that invert on the payoff
Open negative, land positive. Reads as honest rather than advertised-at.

> "Watch everyone say they hate it, until they win it."
> "Imagine hating a toolbox because it has more style than you."

### Direct address to the tribe
"Legends" and "tradies" as a vocative — opening or closing the line, never mid-sentence.
Heaviest in countdown copy.

> "The clock is ticking legends — under 7 days left until our next live draw."
> "It's now or never legends! This ends tonight."

### Aussie jobsite idiom
Smoko, RDO, the shed, the boys, "oi". Used as the attention device, not as decoration.

> "Are you on smoko — come check this out."
> "RDO = Rostered Day Off… or is it Rewards Day On?"
> "Whether it's for the shed, the garage, the workshop or just to flex on the boys, this toolbox is hard to ignore."

### Tool puns and pop-culture riffs
Carries a double meaning back to the product.

> "This is not a drill."
> "Stop being a tool — and listen to this."
> "Welcome to Milwaukee Heaven."

### Possession visualisation
Put the prize in the reader's actual space. This is the workhorse body beat.

> "Imagine all of this, in your garage at home."
> "…and this pink beauty could be rolling into your garage."

### Personification of the hero item
The toolbox as "she". Flirt copy, used sparingly and only on the storage piece.

> "She's pink, she's stacked, and she's got 288 reasons to make your shed jealous."

### Internet vernacular in a trade ad
The deliberate register clash is the joke. Don't overuse — one per script, max.

> "Finally, a toolbox with main character energy."
> "…just because your tools deserve a glow-up."

### Scale superlatives over specs
"Monster", "massive", "biggest". The number lands *after* the feeling.

> "Imagine taking home this massive Milwaukee setup! A monster toolbox, a 13 piece power tool kit and an 8 piece packout system."

### The countdown ladder
Same offer, shrinking clock, gate metaphor instead of a date. Sequence that shipped:
**7 days → 48 hours → 24 hours → drawing tomorrow night → final hours → ends tonight.**

> "Time is almost up — get in before the gates close."
> "The gates are almost shut."

### Retargeting opener
Assumes prior exposure. Cheap and effective for a second-look audience.

> "Still thinking about all of this?"

### The close
Always second person, always ends on ownership. Two shapes carried nearly every script:

> "Click the link, grab a package, and this pink beauty could be yours."
> "So give yourself the best run at it — hit the link, grab a package."

Note **"grab a package"** is the correct CTA: the *pack* is the purchasable unit. Never
"grab entries" / "buy entries" (rule 11 Rule B).

## 3. Say this, not that

The scripts already contain their own fix: **"taking home"** does the job that probability
framing was doing, with none of the legal exposure. Prefer it.

| ❌ Never | ✅ Use instead |
|---|---|
| "the odds couldn't be better" | "someone's about to start their month fully loaded" |
| "your chance to win X" | "you could be **taking home** X" |
| "we're giving you the chance to win…" | "we're **giving away**…" / "win…" |
| "give yourself the chance to…" | "give yourself the best run at it" |
| "last chance to get in" | "last call" / "final hours" / "the gates close tonight" |
| "our next lucky winner" | "our next winner" |
| "one lucky tradie" | "one tradie" |
| "someone's going to win — might as well be you" | "someone's going to win this. It could be you." |
| "boost/increase your chances" | "**more entries**" / "{n}× entries" |
| "buy / purchase entries", "$X per entry" | "the $25 Apprentice pack **includes** 3 free entries" |
| "lottery", "lotto", "raffle", "sweepstake", "gamble", "bet" | "giveaway", "prize draw", "live draw" |

**Watch "chance" specifically.** It is the most frequent contaminant in the source scripts
— it reads as harmless English but lands as probability framing. `odds`, `lottery`,
`raffle`, `sweepstake`, `gamble`, and `bet` are hard-banned outright.

**Also watch "lucky."** Not on rule 11's literal banned list, but "lucky winner" frames the
draw as fortune rather than a free entry into a trade promotion. Drop the adjective; it
adds nothing.

### The free-entry inclusion

Across all three months the ad scripts say "grab a package" but **never once state that
entries are included free** — the word "entries" does not appear anywhere in the August
month. That's a missed opportunity as much as a compliance gap: the free inclusion is the
actual offer.

When a surface has room for it, say it plainly:

> "Every pack **includes** free entries into the monthly draw."

## 4. How the scripts are built

Useful if you're ever generating campaign copy rather than UI strings — and a genuinely
good model for how to structure reusable microcopy.

- **A numbered line bank, not standalone scripts.** Every spoken line has a global id
  (May 1–101, Aug 101–159). Scripts are *assembled* by citing ids, so one edit propagates
  across every video using that line. August line `145` appears in nine scripts.
- **Colour = provenance.** Red numbers are new copy for that script; blue are verbatim
  reuses of an earlier line.
- **Four beats per script:** `HOOK → PRIZE STACK → OBJECTION FLIP (cash alternative) → CTA`.
  The objection flip is optional; the other three are not.
- **Sectioned by intent:** Evergreen → Milestone days (Mother's Day, RDO) → Countdown, plus
  a variant block re-shooting earlier hooks in new setups (`- selfie`, `- in toolbox`).
- Production notes are inline and terse: `B Roll`, `- selfie`, `Insert B roll`.

An audit of all three found **2 hard violations and 25 borderline lines**. Nothing from
those lines has been carried into this document.

  "someone's going to win — might as well be you".

reusing it.** That contamination is exactly why this curated doc exists.
