# iGoDirect / MyRewards portal — UX audit (2026-07-31)

Walked live on production at `https://myrewards.toolsaustralia.com.au` through the real SSO
hand-off as a **Tradie tier, 50% catalogue** member, on desktop (1440x900) and mobile (390x844).
Lock state was determined by fetching each offer page with the member session and testing for
the access-level banner — not by eye. Read-only: nothing was redeemed, purchased or edited.

> The portal is a **white-label product we do not control**. Findings marked iGoDirect can only
> be fixed by the vendor; the ones marked **Tools Australia** need nobody's permission.

## Scores

| Dimension | Score |
|---|---|
| Entitlement clarity | 1/10 |
| Trust & brand coherence | 2/10 |
| Mobile | 2/10 |
| Information architecture | 3/10 |
| Accessibility | 2/10 |
| Commercial effectiveness | 2/10 |
| **Overall** | **2/10** |

## The measured problem

| Surface | Locked | Total | Share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Home page clickable tiles | 13 | 19 | **68%** |
| Hero carousel slides | 3 | 4 | **75%** |
| "Trending stores" row | 3 | 4 | 75% |
| "Gift Cards" row | 3 | 4 | 75% |
| eGift Cards category listing | 9 | 15 | 60% |
| "Popular Offers" row | 2 | 4 | 50% |
| **All 11 categories, first page** | **82** | **155** | **53%** |

The last row is the control: 53% locked is exactly what a 50% tier should look like, and our own
`PARTNER_CATALOG_TIER_COUNTS` agrees (917 of 1,833 offers open at Tradie). **The gating is
correct.** What is broken is that the portal merchandises the locked half harder than the open
half — and marks none of it. The hero banner ("JB HI FI BUSINESS - SHOP NOW") is locked, and on
mobile that locked hero is the entire first screen.

Other measurements: the member tier ("Tradie", "50%") appears **nowhere** in the portal;
Milwaukee / DeWalt / Makita / Ryobi return **zero** results; 39 of 53 images have no alt text;
no `<h1>`; 40 of 62 mobile tap targets are under 44px; three dead links including the
"Offers Near Me" CTA and the "Your Points : 0" header block.

## Verdict

The evidence supports it. On the one job the portal exists to do — showing a paying member what their membership opens — it scores 1: locked and unlocked offers are pixel-identical in every grid, carousel and search result; the member's tier is never displayed on any page; there is no filter, sort or count that would let them enumerate their own benefit; and 68% of home tiles, 75% of hero slides and 60% of the biggest category are refusals. The hero is a locked offer with a SHOP NOW button, and on mobile that locked hero IS the entire fold. On top of that the locked Good Guys page contradicts its own warning ("you now have access") and hands out the merchant's phone number, the footer sends a member asking who holds their data to "igopayments.com.au", and the binding T&Cs call them a "My Rewards Plus" member. I land at 2/10, not 1 — the extra point is for things that verifiably work and are worth protecting: the SSO hand-off is reliable and never asks for a second login, the gating is correctly enforced server-side (the logic is right, only the surfacing fails), the catalogue is real and substantial (1,833 offers, 917 genuinely open at Tradie), search works for category-level terms, and the page loads in about two seconds. The gap between 2 and a defensible 7 is mostly template and config work the vendor already has the data for — which is the argument to take to them.

## The one thing

Render entitlement on the card, everywhere — not on the detail page after a click. Every tile, hero slide, category result and search result must show whether this member can use the offer, using the lock flag the platform already resolves to drive the detail-page warning; locked cards get a lock pill, the level that opens them, and de-emphasis, and the hero carousel stops rotating offers the member cannot redeem. That one template change turns 13 refusals per home page into 6 honest invitations plus 13 priced upgrade prompts, and it is the difference between "I paid for this and can't use it" and "I'm on the 50% plan, here's what 75% adds."

## Findings

### 01. Locked and unlocked offers render identically in every grid, carousel and search result — entitlement is only revealed after a click, on 68% of home tiles

**🔴 critical** · owner: iGoDirect · lens: entitlement + commercial + ia

- **Evidence** — 13 of 19 clickable home tiles (68%) are locked for this Tradie/50% member with zero visual differentiation. In the captures, 'The Good Guys Commercial' (locked) sits at position 1 of Popular Offers with the same card chrome, the same DISCOUNTS pill and the same favourite heart as 'BOGOHO Rewards' (unlocked) at position 3. In Trending stores, Coles eGift and Amazon.com.au eGift (both locked) are indistinguishable from Greenwood Pharmacy (unlocked). In Deals of Week the Sheridan banner (locked) renders a 'REDEEM NOW' button identical to CAT Workwear (unlocked). the captures shows the same in category listings: 9 of 15 eGift tiles locked, every one captioned 'Get 4% Discount', with regex-confirmed zero lock wording anywhere on the page. Lock state appears only on the detail page, as a warning banner.
- **Impact** — At 68% locked, a member browsing at random is refused on roughly two of every three clicks — about three clicks to reach the first offer they can actually use. The imperative captions ('Get 4% Discount', 'REDEEM NOW', 'SHOP NOW') turn every locked card into a promise the platform then breaks. Because the page carries TA's logo and the member pays TA monthly, the resentment lands on TA's membership, not on iGoDirect's platform. This is the mechanical cause of the owner's complaint and of the 1/10.
- **Fix** — Render entitlement state on the card component in every surface — home tiles, hero slides, category grids, search results, See-all views and the recirculation row — not only on the detail page. Locked cards get: (a) a persistent lock glyph or 'Unlock' pill top-right; (b) a label naming the level that opens it ('Unlocks at 75%'); (c) de-emphasis (greyscale logo, 60% opacity); (d) the value caption reworded from 'Get 4% Discount' to 'Unlock 4% Discount' so no promise is made to someone who cannot claim it. The lock flag already exists server-side — it is the same value that drives the detail-page warning — so this is a template change, not new logic. Acceptance: on a 50% account, every locked tile is visually distinguishable from an unlocked one without a click, on every listing template.

### 02. TA's own doorstep copy overpromises what the 50% tier and this catalogue deliver — 'See every deal' and 'of Australia's top tool brands' — two strings TA can ship today

**🔴 critical** · owner: **Tools Australia** · lens: commercial

- **Evidence** — src/components/sections/rewards/RewardsPartnerCard.tsx line 148 renders the portal CTA subline 'See every deal · signed in automatically' on an account that can use 50% of the catalogue and lands on a home page that is 68% locked. Line 75 of the same file renders 'of Australia's top tool brands, on your account' under the 50% dial, while live catalogue searches return milwaukee 0, dewalt 0, makita 0, ryobi 0. Both strings are visible in the captures and the captures Front-page portal merchandising is Hoyts cinemas, BOGOHO cruises, Sheridan bed linen and Dharma Bums activewear.
- **Impact** — TA is manufacturing its own disappointment. These two strings set an expectation the vendor cannot meet, so every lock the member hits reads as TA having misled them rather than as a tier boundary they already knew about. It is the cheapest fix on this entire list — TA's own front-end, no vendor dependency — and it converts the same journey from 'they lied to me' into 'I'm on the 50% plan, fair enough'.
- **Fix** — Two string changes in src/components/sections/rewards/RewardsPartnerCard.tsx (desktop and mobile share the component). (1) Line 148: 'See every deal · signed in automatically' → 'Signed in automatically · Tradie opens 50% of the catalogue'. (2) Line 75: 'of Australia's top tool brands, on your account' → 'of the partner catalogue, on your account' — reinstate the tool-brand claim only when the vendor actually carries tool brands. Then add one expectation line under the dial: 'You'll see the whole catalogue in the portal. Offers above your level show an upgrade prompt.' Also audit the sibling strings that make the same claim: src/components/sections/dashboard/PartnerPreview.tsx line 44, src/components/sections/dashboard/DashboardGuestPanel.tsx line 25, src/components/sections/promo/UnlockDiscounts.tsx line 141.

### 03. The hero — the largest element on the page and the entire mobile fold — advertises an offer this member cannot use, with a purchase-intent 'SHOP NOW' CTA; 3 of 4 hero slides are locked

**🔴 critical** · owner: iGoDirect · lens: entitlement + mobile

- **Evidence** — 3 of 4 hero carousel slides (75%) are locked: JB HiFi Business (1065496), Hoyts Cinemas Standard (1066543) and The Good Guys Commercial (1045580). Only Supercheap Auto eGift (1065007) is usable. The locked JB Hi-Fi slide carries 'JB HI FI BUSINESS · Exclusive discounts across the JB Hi-Fi range' and a yellow 'SHOP NOW' button. At 390×844 the entire above-the-fold view is: TA logo, a 37×38px hamburger, that locked hero, and the red search block — zero offer cards and zero unlocked content in the first 844px.
- **Impact** — On the device most of these members use, the first thing the paid benefit does is invite them to shop and then refuse them. There is no second chance in the fold — no other offer is on screen to recover the impression. It converts the hand-off moment, the point of highest intent seconds after TA's branded takeover, into a knock-back.
- **Fix** — Personalise hero rotation per entitlement: populate hero slots only from the signed-in member's unlocked set, using the tier already passed at SSO. If the vendor will not filter, then at minimum reorder so slide 1 is always an unlocked offer AND render the lock badge from finding 1 on the slide, with the CTA verb changed from 'SHOP NOW' to 'See how to unlock'. Ticket acceptance: on a 50% account, zero locked offers appear above the fold on desktop or mobile, and no unentitled offer ever renders a purchase-intent CTA.

### 04. The member's tier is never displayed anywhere in the portal, and the only member-status slot in the header is a dead-link 'Your Points : 0 / Your Savings : $0.00' — absent altogether on mobile

**🔴 critical** · owner: both · lens: entitlement + trust

- **Evidence** — Regex probes across /v8/home, /v8/my-account and a locked offer page: 'Tradie' never appears; '50% catalogue' never appears as a member-status statement; 'access level' appears only inside the locked-offer warning, and even there it never says what the level is. Contrast the captures, where TA's own hand-off reads 'Tradie · 50% catalogue · Single-use sign-on link · expires in 60 min' seconds before the portal loads. The only status element in the portal header is 'Your Points : 0 / Your Savings : $0.00' (the captures, the captures, the captures) — an `<a href="">` dead link — and on mobile the whole header block is replaced by a hamburger, so mobile members get no status indicator at all. The unlocked BOGOHO page gives no positive entitlement confirmation either, just a bare 'Redeem Now'.
- **Impact** — TA does the expensive work of establishing tier and entitlement, then the portal immediately forgets it — the member can only infer their entitlement from click-by-click refusals. On a locked offer they are scolded; on an unlocked offer they are told nothing. And the one piece of header real estate reserved for member status is occupied by a permanent zero, so the portal actively signals 'you have nothing' to someone paying monthly.
- **Fix** — VENDOR: extend the SSO payload contract to carry tier_label and catalogue_pct, and replace the Points/Savings header block for the TA tenant with a persistent member-status chip on BOTH desktop and mobile — 'Tradie · 917 of 1,833 offers unlocked' — linking to a 'what my tier opens' listing. Add a positive line above the Redeem button on every unlocked offer: 'Included with your Tradie membership.' TA: pass tier_label and catalogue_pct in the hand-off payload now, and make the tier line a persistent panel on /my-account/rewards rather than a footnote on a self-dismissing transit screen. TA can ship its half unilaterally — PARTNER_CATALOG_TIER_COUNTS in src/generated/partnerCatalogPreview.ts already holds 917 at 50%, 1,375 at 75%, 1,833 at 100% (PARTNER_CATALOG_TOTAL = 1833).

### 05. There is no 'available to me' filter, sort or result count anywhere — the only way a member can enumerate their own benefit is to open offers one at a time, across four pages

**🔴 critical** · owner: iGoDirect · lens: entitlement + ia + commercial

- **Evidence** — The only control on a category listing is a 'Sort by' dropdown with four options — Most popular / A–Z / Z–A / Latest. There is no filter control of any kind, and no 'available to me' sort key. On page 1 of eGift Cards, 9 of 15 tiles are locked, so the member must open all 15 detail pages to learn which 6 work; pagination reads 1 2 3 4 with no 'showing 1–15 of N' anywhere on the page. Search results were regex-checked and contain zero lock or upgrade wording. Search URLs are unshareable CakePHP query strings (/products/modern_search?data[Product][keywords]=…), so a member cannot even bookmark a set they have manually verified.
- **Impact** — There is no path — none — by which a paying member can answer 'what does my membership actually get me?' inside the product that exists to answer it. Click-to-value on the biggest category runs at roughly one in two and a half, across four pages. This is the difference between a perk a member opens monthly and one they try once and abandon, and TA pays for that churn.
- **Fix** — Add a persistent 'Available on my membership' toggle, DEFAULTED ON, above every category grid, the search results page and every See-all view, with a matching 'Locked' facet for members who want to browse upgrade value. Default-on is the ticketable requirement — an off-by-default filter leaves the dishonest view as the landing state. Add 'Available to me' as a fifth sort key. Print a count line above every grid: 'Showing 6 of 15 available on Tradie — 9 more with an upgrade', where the trailing clause links to the upgrade prompt. Add a filter rail (category, online vs in-store, discount type, discount value).

### 06. All four power-tool brands TA advertises return zero results, and the home page and nav merchandise consumer lifestyle over trade — CAT Workwear is the only trade offer on the page

**🔴 critical** · owner: both · lens: ia + commercial

- **Evidence** — Live catalogue keyword search: milwaukee = 0, dewalt = 0, makita = 0, ryobi = 0 (the captures shows the explicit 'No results found' page). By contrast tools = 8, trade = 15, ute = 15, fuel = 7, workwear = 6, hardware = 4, bunnings = 3, safety boots = 1. the captures merchandises Hoyts cinema tickets, BOGOHO cruises, Sheridan bed linen (the single largest tile on the page) and Dharma Bums activewear. Of the 13 unique offers on the home page, exactly one — CAT Workwear, 4% cashback — is trade merchandise. None of the 11 nav categories (Featured Offers, Automotive, Body & Soul, Cashback Offers, Eat and Drink, eGift Cards, Home & Lifestyle, In-store Offers, Leisure & Entertainment, Shop, Travel) is a trade, tools, workwear or hardware shelf.
- **Impact** — TA sells a tools membership and hands the member into a general consumer lifestyle catalogue. A tradie's first instinct is to search their own tool brand, and all four of those searches dead-end inside the first thirty seconds — which reads as 'this benefit is not for me'. It devalues the partner-discount pillar of every tier and quietly kills the tier-upgrade story TA sells on top of it.
- **Fix** — VENDOR: source and load a trade/industrial merchant set — tool retail, workwear and PPE, fuel and fleet, tyres and vehicle servicing, building supplies, trade insurance, phone/data plans, tool finance — and add a 'Trade & Tools' top-level category to the nav strip. Where a brand genuinely has no offer, return a branded 'we're working on {brand}' state with a 'request this merchant' capture, and report captured brand demand to TA monthly. Put a written merchant-acquisition commitment and date against this. TA: until it lands, publish the real merchant list on /my-account/rewards before the hand-off and lead the takeover copy with the categories that do deliver today (fuel, automotive/ute, workwear, eGift cards) rather than 'partner discounts' in the abstract.

### 07. The locked Good Guys page contradicts its own lock warning three lines later — 'you now have access' — instructs the member to click a Redeem button the page does not render, and publishes the merchant's phone and email

**🟠 major** · owner: iGoDirect · lens: entitlement + trust + ia

- **Evidence** — In the captures, three lines below 'This offer isn't unlocked at your current access level', the body copy reads 'As a valued My Rewards Plus member, you now have access to The Good Guys Commercial Corporate Benefits website…'. It then supplies redemption instructions — 'Click "Redeem now" and this will automatically log you into the new Corporate Benefits website' — for a button that is not present anywhere on the page, plus a live merchant support line: Phone 1300 22 55 64, Email buyinggroups@thegoodguys.com.au. The same pattern appears on the locked JB Hi-Fi page: 'Don't miss out on these unbeatable deals—shop now and save big!' rendered directly beneath the lock warning. the captures confirms the red 'Redeem Now' button normally occupies exactly the slot the warning now takes.
- **Impact** — A member who reads the reassuring paragraph rings The Good Guys, says they are a Tools Australia member and is refused at the merchant — a failed redemption that lands on TA's brand, TA's support queue and TA's merchant relationship, not the vendor's. A member who believes the body copy instead concludes the site is broken rather than that they are on a lower tier, so a moment engineered as an upgrade prompt lands as a bug report and TA loses both the sale and the trust.
- **Fix** — On the locked template, suppress the merchant blob entirely or collapse it behind a disclosure labelled 'Preview what this unlocks'. Strip from the locked view: all redemption instructions (hide any block matching the redeem-instruction field), all merchant contact details, and any second-person entitlement phrasing. Replace with one coherent locked state in the CTA slot: offer name, one-line description, the level that opens it, one CTA. Ticket acceptance test: a locked offer page must contain no phone number, no email address, no imperative redemption instruction, and no occurrence of the string 'you now have access'.

### 08. A phantom points economy: a currency TA members can never earn is displayed portal-wide, with a dead-link header and the dashboard's largest, reddest CTA leading into an empty wallet

**🟠 major** · owner: iGoDirect · lens: trust + commercial

- **Evidence** — Every page header shows 'Your Points : 0' and 'Your Savings : $0.00' (the captures, 03, 04, 05, 06, 07). The dashboard makes 'Redeem points as eGift Cards' the largest, reddest CTA on the page, above 'My Total Points / 0' with the fine print '1 point = $1', alongside 'My Total Savings $0.00' and 'My Shopping Cart 0' (the captures, the captures). TA operates no points currency and there is no mechanism by which a TA member can earn a point. The header element is `<a href="">` — one of three dead links on the home page. On mobile the whole block is dropped, taking the only member-status indicator with it.
- **Impact** — It dilutes the one thing the portal is for: attention goes to a currency that does not exist rather than to the discounts the member paid for, and '1 point = $1' quantifies the disappointment — the portal is telling them they hold zero dollars of value. Worse, 'Your Savings : $0.00' is the single number that measures whether the membership is paying for itself, and it is pinned at zero under TA's logo on every page — an anti-testimonial rendered in the member's own account during the exact session where they are deciding whether this was worth it.
- **Fix** — Add a tenant flag (points_module_enabled = false) for the TAR tenant that removes (a) the 'Your Points / Your Savings' header element, (b) the 'My Total Points / 1 point = $1 / Redeem points as eGift Cards' dashboard tile, and (c) 'My Total Savings' and 'My Shopping Cart' while neither can accrue. Replace the freed dashboard slot with a membership tile fed from the SSO payload: tier name, catalogue %, and a link back to toolsaustralia.com.au/my-account/rewards. If per-tenant disabling is not supported, that is itself the escalation — as an interim, hide via tenant config/CSS and confirm in writing that no TA member can be shown a points balance or a $0.00 savings figure. Keep a real savings counter only if the vendor can populate it from actual redemptions (see finding 24).

### 09. On a portal branded end-to-end as Tools Australia, the footer Privacy Policy and Terms links leave to two unexplained vendor domains — igopayments.com.au and myrewardsinternational.com

**🟠 major** · owner: both · lens: trust

- **Evidence** — Footer 'Privacy Policy' → https://igopayments.com.au/privacy-policy/ and footer 'Terms & Conditions' → https://www.myrewardsinternational.com/terms-and-conditions. Both sit directly beneath the Tools Australia logo and '© Tools Australia Rewards (TAR)' on every page (visible in the captures, 03, 04, 05, 06, 07).
- **Impact** — A member who clicks to find out who holds their data is sent to a company called 'iGo Payments' — which, to a customer on a recurring card charge, reads as though their payment details have been passed to an unknown third party. It is the single worst possible destination for that click. It also leaves the member unable to identify the entity that actually collects and handles their information from a page branded end-to-end as Tools Australia.
- **Fix** — VENDOR: make footer Privacy Policy and Terms & Conditions URLs per-tenant configurable and set the TAR tenant's to https://toolsaustralia.com.au/privacy and https://toolsaustralia.com.au/terms. This is a config change, not a build — send the two URLs this week and ask for a date. TA: before those links flip, publish a named sub-section on /privacy — 'Partner discount portal, operated by iGoDirect' — disclosing the vendor as a processor and naming exactly what is shared over SSO, so the disclosure obligation is met on TA's own page rather than by an unexplained offsite link.

### 10. 'Offers Near Me' — the highest-contrast CTA in the header of every page — is a dead link (href=""), 18px tall on mobile, beside a 'Location' label that never resolves

**🟠 major** · owner: iGoDirect · lens: ia + mobile + a11y

In the same ticket, fix the other two dead links.

### 11. The 'Unlock this Offer' CTA renders inconsistently — present on the locked JB Hi-Fi page, entirely absent on the locked Good Guys page, so some refusals offer no next step at all

**🟠 major** · owner: iGoDirect · lens: entitlement

- **Evidence** — The locked JB Hi-Fi page shows a red 'Unlock this Offer' button and a favourite heart inline with the warning, on both desktop and mobile. The locked The Good Guys Commercial page (the captures, offer 1045580) shows the identical warning sentence with no 'Unlock this Offer' button and no heart — the member's only exit is the '‹ Back' link. Same lock state, two different affordances, on the same account in the same session. Catalogue-wide prevalence is unmeasured.
- **Impact** — On the pages missing the CTA, the member hits a refusal with no offered next step — the upgrade path silently disappears at exactly the moment of highest upgrade intent. Every one of those impressions is a conversion TA paid to create and cannot capture.
- **Fix** — Make the unlock CTA a mandatory, server-rendered element of the locked-offer template rather than a conditionally injected one, and add a regression check that no offer page can render the lock warning without an accompanying CTA. Vendor to confirm which condition suppressed it on offer 1045580 and report back how many catalogue offers are affected.

### 12. The upgrade prompt names no tier, no next level, no price and no delta — an un-priced blind ask fired 13 times on the home page alone

**🟠 major** · owner: both · lens: entitlement + commercial

- **Evidence** — The full warning reads: 'This offer isn't unlocked at your current access level. Upgrade your membership or grab a one-time pack at Tools Australia to unlock more of the partner catalogue.' with a single red 'Unlock this Offer' button set mid-sentence. It names no current level, no required level, no price and no count. Regex probes confirm 'Tradie' never appears anywhere in the portal, and 'access level' appears only in this string. TA renders the equivalent moment on its own side as 'You're at 50% — JB HiFi Business needs 75%' with a cheapest-covering-plan recommendation (src/utils/partner-discounts/portal-return.ts, unlock-packages.ts), and holds a per-offer access threshold for all 1,833 offers in src/generated/partnerCatalogOffers.ts — data the vendor supplied, so the vendor holds it too and simply does not render it.
- **Impact** — An upgrade ask with no numbers is a blind ask. The member cannot judge whether upgrading is worth it, because they are never told how much more catalogue they would get. It reads as an arbitrary paywall rather than a tier, it makes TA's pricing look capricious, and it converts at a fraction of what it could at the highest-intent moment TA gets — a member who has just told you exactly which offer they want.
- **Fix** — VENDOR: template the banner to render, in order — (a) the member's level by name and size: 'Your Tradie access covers 917 of 1,833 offers'; (b) what this offer gives; (c) the exact next step from TA-supplied values: '{next tier} · ${price}/mo opens {n}% of the catalogue including this offer'; (d) a scope number: '+{n} more offers on this page open too'. Keep the TA-authored CTA wording tenant-configurable — TA carries legal exposure on how its packs are described and this string must never be edited by the vendor without TA sign-off. TA: build an offer-aware landing so the passed offer_id is used — /membership should render the offer's logo and name above the plan table and pre-select the lowest tier that opens it, then fire a distinct UTM-tagged conversion so this surface's yield is measurable. Copy rule for both sides: entries are a free inclusion with the membership or pack — never priced per entry, never framed as buying entries.

### 13. TA's own /my-account/rewards names the 50% figure but never names a single unlocked offer, stacks two unrelated discount programmes under one heading, and the post-logout return drops the member on the TA homepage

**🟠 major** · owner: **Tools Australia** · lens: commercial + entitlement

- **Evidence** — TA renders the exact entitlement twice on the way out — 'Tradie · 50% catalogue · Single-use sign-on link · expires in 60 min' in the SSO takeover and 'Catalogue unlocked / 50%' plus 'PARTNER DISCOUNT QUEUE — Tradie · 50% active' on the rewards page (the captures, the captures) — but never names one usable offer. The same page shows four TA-direct partner tiles (ZJWRAPS, Super Bad, Multi Hub, All Round Trade Constructions) under the same 'PARTNER DISCOUNTS' heading as the portal button, none of which exist in the portal catalogue. The seven confirmed-unlocked portal offers (Supercheap Auto eGift, BOGOHO Rewards, MyDriver Australia, Choice eGift Card, CAT Workwear, Dharma Bums, Greenwood Pharmacy) appear nowhere on TA's side. Portal Log Out was tested: it lands the member on the TA homepage, not back on /my-account/rewards where they started.
- **Impact** — TA is handing over the only page where it controls the narrative without using it. Two unrelated discount programmes sit under one heading with no explanation of which is which, and the member crosses into the portal with no idea what 'their' half looks like — so the first lock they hit defines the whole experience. Every entitlement fact surfaced before the jump is a lock the member is no longer surprised by, and it costs nothing but TA's own front-end time.
- **Fix** — Rebuild /my-account/rewards as the entitlement pre-frame, TA-side only. (1) Split the two programmes visually and by label — 'Tools Australia partners' for the four TA tiles, 'Partner rewards portal' as its own block with the portal CTA — so they are never read as one list. (2) Under the 50% dial, name what is open right now: a horizontal strip of confirmed-unlocked offers with logos and a 'See all in the portal' link, seeded from a curated monthly list until the vendor exposes an entitlement endpoint. (3) Add the expectation line from finding 2. (4) Add a tier-upgrade card showing exactly what the next level opens, written once and reused as the copy TA supplies to the vendor for the offer-page banner, so the two surfaces never drift. (5) Set the post-logout return URL to /my-account/rewards and point the portal's 'Tools AU Account' nav link at /my-account/rewards rather than /my-account.

### 14. Offer body copy and binding T&Cs address the member as a 'My Rewards Plus' member — the vendor's brand, not the one they pay — and assets are served from an /igodirect/ path under an invented 'TAR' initialism

**🟠 major** · owner: iGoDirect · lens: trust

## What already works

- TA's own SSO hand-off is genuinely excellent and is the best-designed moment in the whole journey. The branded takeover names the product, shows a three-step checklist, gives a Cancel escape, and states the entitlement in plain words: 'Tradie · 50% catalogue · Single-use sign-on link · expires in 60 min'. It sets the standard the portal fails to meet — the review's spine is that gap, not a shortage of good thinking on TA's side.
- Sign-on works and works quietly. The member is never asked for a second login, the token flow is single-use with a 60-minute expiry, and Log Out is recoverable even though it drops them on the wrong page. For a white-label SSO integration, this is the part most often broken and here it is not.
- The gating logic is correct — it is only the surfacing that fails. Locked offers really are locked, the entitlement check runs server-side on every detail page, and the warning it produces is TA-authored and legally clean: 'Upgrade your membership or grab a one-time pack' contains no gambling framing and does not price or sell entries. The data and the rules exist; they are simply not rendered anywhere a member can act on them.
- The catalogue is real and substantial, not a shell. 1,833 offers, 917 genuinely open at Tradie, and several are a good fit for this audience — Supercheap Auto eGift, CAT Workwear at 4% cashback, MyDriver Australia, plus BCF, Rebel and Bunnings hits. Category-level search returns sensible counts (trade 15, ute 15, tools 8, fuel 7, workwear 6). The merchandising is wrong for a trade brand, but the underlying inventory is worth fixing rather than replacing.
- The tenancy theming layer exists and is already applying TA's logo, red palette and a tools-themed dashboard hero image. That matters commercially: it means several of the highest-value asks — legal link URLs, the points module, the member label, design tokens — are plausibly config or template changes rather than platform builds, which is the strongest argument for demanding dates rather than a roadmap.
- Performance is acceptable and not the problem: DOMContentLoaded around 1.2s and load around 2.0s across 64 requests. The site is also genuinely responsive rather than a desktop layout squeezed onto a phone, and lang="en" and the viewport meta are both present.
- The favourites heart is on every card and there is a Favourites view in the account nav. That is exactly the raw material for a 'my offers' surface — the shortest path from the current product to something a member would return to monthly, if entitlement were visible on the card first.
- Search, sort and pagination all function, and the offer detail template has a clear two-column shape with images, thumbnails and terms. The bones of a competent catalogue are there; what is missing is the one layer that makes it a membership benefit rather than a directory.
